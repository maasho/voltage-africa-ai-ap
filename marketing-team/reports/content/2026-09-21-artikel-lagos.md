Finding an electrician in Lagos is easy. Finding one you can trust with your wiring, your inverter and your family's safety is harder. Lagos runs on informal referrals — "my brother knows a guy" — and while many artisans are genuinely skilled, the city also has its share of burnt inverter boards, undersized cables and "electricians" who vanish after collecting a deposit.

The good news: Nigeria actually has a formal certification system for electrical workers, and you can check it yourself in minutes. This guide shows you how — plus the practical checks that no certificate can replace.

## Who licenses electricians in Nigeria? Meet NEMSA

The body that matters is **NEMSA — the Nigerian Electricity Management Services Agency**. Established by the NEMSA Act of 2015, with its mandate now anchored in the Electricity Act 2023, NEMSA is the sole statutory agency empowered to enforce technical standards and to test and certify electrical installations and the people who carry them out (Source: NEMSA certification guidelines, nemsa.gov.ng).

When an electrician says "I'm certified", this is what they should mean: NEMSA assesses electrical installation contractors and technical personnel through **written and oral competency tests** and then issues a **competency certificate** in categories ranging from individual technician classes up to corporate certification. Certificates are valid for a set period (NEMSA's guidelines state six years) and successful candidates receive a certificate plus a seal or stamp according to their category (Source: NEMSA "Guidelines and Procedures for Certification of Corporate Companies and Individual Electrical Installation Contractors", nemsa.gov.ng).

## The certificate most people forget: the installation itself

Here is the part that surprises many homeowners. NEMSA certifies **two different things**:

1. **The person** — the competency certificate described above.
2. **The installation** — new or substantially modified electrical installations must be inspected and tested by NEMSA and issued a Certificate of Inspection before they are legally energised and put into use (Source: InfraForce Africa compliance guide, July 2026; NEMSA mandate documents).

So a certified electrician can still produce an installation that was never inspected. For anything beyond small repairs — a full house rewire, a new distribution board, an inverter or solar installation — ask how the NEMSA inspection will be handled. NEMSA has also issued specific safety guidelines for rooftop solar installations under the Electricity Act 2023, after linking a rise in fire incidents to poor workmanship and substandard equipment (Source: The Electricity Hub, April 2026).

## How to actually verify an electrician, step by step

1. **Ask for the NEMSA competency certificate** — the document, not a verbal claim. Note the certificate number, the category and the expiry date.
2. **Check it online.** NEMSA maintains an online portal where you can verify a Certificate ID, and a public directory of certified electrical contractors (Source: Elsan Transformers guide to NEMSA, 2026, describing nemsa.gov.ng verification). No listing, no deal — or at least, a long conversation about why.
3. **Match the identity.** The name on the certificate should match the person or company quoting you, and a photo ID should back it up. A borrowed certificate is a known trick.
4. **Check the scope fits the job.** Higher categories cover heavier work; a residential technician certificate does not qualify someone for your factory's transformer installation.
5. **Ask for two or three recent references** — real customers you can call, ideally with photos of finished work.

## Where does COREN fit in?

You will also hear about **COREN — the Council for the Regulation of Engineering in Nigeria**. COREN registers engineers, and Nigerian law requires engineering design and supervision to be signed off by COREN-registered engineers. In practice, most day-to-day residential wiring in Lagos is done by technicians and artisans — who may hold trade-test certificates or have served apprenticeships — while the design of distribution boards, earthing systems and protection coordination for larger projects should involve a COREN-registered engineer (Source: Nigeria Building Cost Estimator, 2026).

The simple rule: for a repair or a room extension, a NEMSA-certified electrician is your check. For a new build, a commercial premises or a large solar system, ask who the supervising engineer is — and their COREN number.

## Materials: the silent half of the job

Even a perfect installation fails with fake materials. Substandard and counterfeit cables — with less copper than the label claims — are a documented problem in Nigerian markets. Ask which brands will be used, look for the **SON (Standards Organisation of Nigeria)** mark and IEC ratings on breakers, and insist on receipts from authorised dealers. Inspect materials before they go into your walls. "Same same, just cheaper" is how fires start.

## Money: quotes, deposits and fair payment

Prices in Lagos vary with the job, the materials, the area and the season — be sceptical of anyone quoting a firm price without seeing the site. What you can and should fix in advance is the **structure**: a written quote that separates labour and materials, a modest deposit (typically for materials), and the balance after completion and your inspection. Full cash payment upfront is the single most common prelude to a disappearing act.

## Red flags — walk away if you hear these

- "You don't need papers for a small job like this."
- Cannot produce a NEMSA certificate number — or gets defensive when asked.
- Verbal-only pricing, or a price that grows mysteriously once work starts.
- Full payment demanded upfront, in cash.
- Unbranded cable, no receipts, refusal to let you inspect materials.
- References that turn out to be "my cousin can confirm".
